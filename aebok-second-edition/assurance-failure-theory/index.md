## Why Assurance Failure Theory?

Failures rarely begin with catastrophic events.

They usually begin with degraded assurance.

Assurance Failure Theory™ studies how Operational Trust deteriorates, how assurance failures emerge, how they propagate, and how they can be prevented.

Rather than asking why a system failed, Assurance Engineering asks why trust in the system failed.

---

# Definition

Assurance Failure Theory™ is the study of the mechanisms, patterns, dynamics, and consequences of assurance degradation across regulated systems.

Failures may occur because of:

- Missing evidence
- Incorrect evidence
- Delayed evidence
- Conflicting evidence
- Human error
- AI error
- Control failure
- Process deviation
- Environmental change

---

# Cascading Assurance Failure

One assurance failure may trigger many others.

Examples:

- Supplier issue
- Validation impact
- Manufacturing delay
- Batch release delay
- Regulatory observation

The Assurance Graph™ enables these cascades to be visualized.

---

# Weak Assurance Signals

Most assurance failures begin as weak signals.

Examples include:

- increasing exceptions
- overdue reviews
- repeated overrides
- incomplete documentation
- unusual AI behavior
- recurring manual workarounds

The goal is to identify these signals before Operational Trust declines significantly.
