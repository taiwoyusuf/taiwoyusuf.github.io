## Why Assurance Physics?

Engineering disciplines seek to understand not only what systems do, but how they behave over time.

Assurance Engineering extends this thinking to Operational Trust.

Assurance Physics™ studies the observable behavior of assurance within complex operational systems.

Rather than treating trust as a static score, Assurance Physics™ examines how trust emerges, changes, propagates, stabilizes, and recovers through measurable interactions.

---

# Definition

Assurance Physics™ is the study of the dynamic behavior of Operational Trust within interconnected assurance systems.

It provides conceptual models that describe how assurance behaves under changing operational conditions.

---

# Assurance Energy™

Organizations expend effort to establish and maintain trust.

Examples include:

- validation activities
- training
- inspections
- cybersecurity controls
- preventive maintenance
- evidence collection

Assurance Energy™ represents the organizational effort invested in creating and sustaining Operational Trust.

---

# Assurance Momentum™

Well-governed systems often continue performing reliably because of sustained operational discipline.

Assurance Momentum™ describes the tendency of mature assurance systems to maintain trustworthy operation unless disrupted.

---

# Assurance Friction™

Operational Trust may be reduced by organizational resistance.

Examples include:

- manual workarounds
- duplicated processes
- poor communication
- fragmented evidence
- inconsistent governance

Assurance Friction™ increases the effort required to maintain Operational Trust.

---

# Assurance Inertia™

Organizations often continue existing assurance practices even when better methods become available.

Assurance Inertia™ represents resistance to assurance transformation.

---

# Assurance Resonance™

Independent governance improvements may reinforce one another.

Examples:

- better validation
- improved cybersecurity
- stronger change control
- clearer accountability

Together these may produce assurance improvements greater than the sum of individual changes.
