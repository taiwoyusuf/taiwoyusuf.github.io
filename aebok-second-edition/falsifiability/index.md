# Falsifiability in Assurance Engineering™

# Chapter 59

---

## Why Falsifiability?

Scientific disciplines advance by proposing explanations that can be tested and potentially disproven.

Assurance Engineering™ adopts the same principle.

The objective is not to defend theories indefinitely but to strengthen or replace them through evidence.

---

# Definition

Falsifiability in Assurance Engineering™ is the principle that every scientific theory, model, metric, or engineering claim should identify the observations or experiments that could demonstrate its limitations or incorrectness.

---

# Core Principle

Confidence grows when ideas survive rigorous attempts to refute them.

---

# Examples

Operational Trust™ models should identify:

- assumptions
- operating limits
- failure conditions
- conflicting evidence

Predictive Assurance™ models should specify:

- prediction accuracy
- uncertainty
- validation criteria

Assurance Instruments™ should define:

- calibration limits
- measurement uncertainty
- environmental constraints

---

# Scientific Responsibilities

Researchers should clearly document:

- hypotheses
- assumptions
- limitations
- expected outcomes
- unexpected outcomes
- conditions requiring revision

---

# Learning Through Refutation

Negative experimental results contribute valuable scientific knowledge.

Refined theories represent progress rather than failure.

---

# Relationship to the Scientific Method™

Falsifiability complements experimentation by ensuring that experiments are capable of challenging existing knowledge.

---

# Relationship to Assurance Commons™

Results supporting and challenging theories should both be preserved.

---

# Future Evolution

Falsifiability strengthens Assurance Engineering by encouraging continuous improvement through evidence rather than authority.
