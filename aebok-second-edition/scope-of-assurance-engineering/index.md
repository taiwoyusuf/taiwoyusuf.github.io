# Scope of Assurance Engineering™

# Chapter 49

---

## Why Scope Matters

Every engineering discipline defines both what it includes and what it does not include.

Clear boundaries strengthen scientific identity, reduce ambiguity, and improve interdisciplinary collaboration.

Assurance Engineering™ does not replace existing engineering disciplines.

Instead, it complements them by focusing on trustworthy operation.

---

# Definition

The Scope of Assurance Engineering™ defines the scientific, engineering, operational, and organizational problems addressed by the discipline.

---

# What Assurance Engineering Studies

Assurance Engineering studies:

- Operational Trust™
- Assurance States™
- Assurance Integrity™
- Decision Confidence™
- Assurance Intelligence™
- Assurance Evidence™
- Assurance Phenomena™
- Assurance Systems™
- Trustworthy Environments™
- Human-AI Assurance

---

# What Assurance Engineering Does Not Replace

Assurance Engineering does not replace:

- Mechanical Engineering
- Electrical Engineering
- Chemical Engineering
- Computer Science
- Software Engineering
- Cybersecurity
- Quality Engineering
- Systems Engineering
- Regulatory Affairs

These disciplines remain essential.

Assurance Engineering integrates their outputs to support trustworthy operation.

---

# Relationship with Existing Disciplines

Assurance Engineering collaborates with:

- Systems Engineering
- Human Factors Engineering
- Reliability Engineering
- Safety Engineering
- Software Engineering
- AI Engineering
- Industrial Engineering
- Risk Management
- Regulatory Science

Each contributes expertise that Assurance Engineering incorporates into a broader assurance perspective.

---

# Primary Engineering Question

The defining question of Assurance Engineering is:

> Can this system be responsibly trusted to operate under its intended conditions, and what evidence supports that conclusion?
