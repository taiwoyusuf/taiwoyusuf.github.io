# Trust Propagation Theory™

# Chapter 23

---

## Why Trust Propagation Theory?

Operational Trust rarely exists in isolation.

Modern organizations operate as interconnected networks of Assurance Objects whose trust relationships continuously influence one another.

Trust Propagation Theory™ studies how changes in Operational Trust influence connected objects throughout an assurance ecosystem.

Rather than evaluating trust independently, Assurance Engineering recognizes that trust behaves as a connected system.

---

# Definition

Trust Propagation Theory™ is the study of how Operational Trust changes propagate through Assurance Graphs™ as a consequence of dependencies, evidence changes, operational events, and human decisions.

---

# Core Principle

Trust does not remain local.

Trust propagates.

Every assurance event possesses the potential to influence connected Assurance Objects.

---

# Sources of Propagation

Examples include:

- Validation completion
- Validation failure
- AI model drift
- Supplier quality changes
- Cybersecurity incidents
- CAPA closure
- Backup failure
- Regulatory findings
- Human approval
- Equipment failure

---

# Propagation Paths

Trust may propagate through:

- Technical dependencies
- Operational dependencies
- Organizational dependencies
- Regulatory dependencies
- Human accountability
- AI decision chains
- Supply chains

---

# Positive Propagation

Improved assurance may strengthen connected Operational Trust.

Examples include:

- Successful validation
- CAPA completion
- Supplier certification
- AI retraining
- Cybersecurity improvements

---

# Negative Propagation

Trust degradation may cascade across the Assurance Graph™.

Examples include:

- Shared infrastructure failure
- Compromised identity
- Incorrect AI recommendation
- Regulatory observation
- Failed supplier audit

---

# Trust Dampening™

Not every trust event propagates equally.

Some governance controls reduce propagation.

Examples include:

- Segmentation
- Independent verification
- Human approval
- Redundant controls
- Risk isolation

These controls dampen trust propagation.

---

# Trust Amplification™

Certain operational conditions increase propagation.

Examples include:

- Shared infrastructure
- Shared AI models
- Shared suppliers
- Shared evidence
- Centralized approvals

Amplification increases organizational sensitivity.

---

# Trust Containment™

Organizations should design assurance architectures that limit unnecessary trust propagation.

The objective is not preventing propagation.

The objective is controlling it.
