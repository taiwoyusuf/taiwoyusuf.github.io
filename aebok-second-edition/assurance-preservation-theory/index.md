# Chapter 26

## Why Assurance Preservation Theory?

Operational Trust is built over years but can be lost quickly when evidence, knowledge, accountability, or organizational memory disappear.

Assurance Preservation Theory™ studies how organizations preserve Operational Trust across technological, organizational, and regulatory change.

---

# Definition

Assurance Preservation Theory™ is the study of preserving assurance capability, evidence, organizational memory, and Operational Trust throughout the lifecycle of an enterprise.

---

# Core Principle

Operational Trust should survive change.

Changes in people, technology, suppliers, organizational structure, or regulation should not unnecessarily destroy assurance capability.

---

# Preservation Objects

Organizations preserve:

- Assurance Evidence
- Assurance Twins™
- Assurance Graphs™
- Operational Knowledge
- Human Accountability
- Decision History
- Governance Models
- AI Assurance Records
- Regulatory Evidence

---

# Preservation Threats

Examples include:

- personnel turnover
- obsolete technology
- incomplete migrations
- undocumented decisions
- missing evidence
- supplier transitions
- organizational restructuring
- AI model replacement

---

# Assurance Continuity™

Operational Trust should continue despite operational change.

Continuity requires preserving:

- evidence lineage
- decision lineage
- accountability
- traceability
- institutional knowledge

---

# Assurance Legacy™

Historical assurance information is not merely archived.
